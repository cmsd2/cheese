# Milk selection

What to look for when shopping for milk for cheese-making. Standard supermarket pasteurised homogenised whole milk works, but a few targeted upgrades make a meaningful difference. Priorities ordered by impact on the cheese.

## The big lever: non-homogenised

Homogenisation is the single biggest reversible improvement. It breaks fat globules into tiny uniform particles coated in whey-protein, which:

- Makes the curd network softer and slower to expel whey (wetter cheddaring, wetter finished cheese)
- Changes flavour (more "milky" / less "creamy")
- Slightly higher yield but at the cost of texture

**Look for**: "non-homogenised", "cream-line", "cream-on-top", or a visible cream layer at the top of the bottle.

## The other non-negotiable: pasteurisation method

| Type | Label | Cheese quality |
|---|---|---|
| Raw | "Raw drinking milk", direct from farm | Best — full enzymes, native flora, full Ca |
| Vat / LTLT (63 °C × 30 min) | "Gently pasteurised", "vat pasteurised" | Very good |
| HTST (72 °C × 15 s) | Standard supermarket pasteurised | Workable |
| Ultra-pasteurised | "Extended shelf-life", "fresh-keeping", "longer-lasting" | **Useless** for hard cheese |
| UHT | "UHT", "long-life" | **Useless** |

Anything sitting on the supermarket shelf with a date >10 days out is suspect — check for ultra-pasteurised labelling.

## The flavour/texture upgrade: breed

| Breed | Fat | Protein | Cheese impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holstein/Friesian (standard) | ~3.5 % | ~3.2 % | Neutral, generic |
| **Jersey / Guernsey / Channel Island** | ~4.8–5.2 % | ~3.6–3.8 % | Richer, higher-yielding, golden colour from β-carotene |
| Ayrshire | ~4.0 % | ~3.4 % | Good middle ground |
| Shorthorn | ~3.8 % | ~3.4 % | Traditional UK breed |

**Look for**: "Jersey", "Guernsey", "Channel Island", "Gold Top". This alone bumps yield 1–2 % and gives a noticeably richer cheese.

## The nice-to-haves

- **Grass-fed / pasture-raised**: more flavour complexity, higher CLA/omega-3, deeper colour. Seasonal — spring milk (fresh grass) > late winter (dry feed).
- **Single-farm / "estate"**: consistent breed and herd vs a co-op pool of mixed origin.
- **Organic**: doesn't directly affect cheese quality but correlates strongly with non-homogenised + traditional handling.
- **Freshness**: pick the latest sell-by date you can find. Older milk has more native bacterial activity and weaker casein structure.

## UK buying options, in increasing effort

1. **Waitrose / M&S / Ocado** — stock non-homogenised options. Look for Duchy Organic (Waitrose), Daylesford, Yeo Valley Jersey, Northiam Dairy, Estate Dairy, Ivy House Farm.
2. **Independent dairy doorstep delivery** — e.g. Parker Dairies, Modern Milkman, Milk & More. Many carry Channel Island and non-homogenised in glass bottles, very fresh.
3. **Farmers' markets** — variable but often have a stallholder selling milk from one named farm.
4. **Direct from farm / raw milk** — UK legal with restrictions; search "Raw Drinking Milk near [postcode]" or use the Food Standards Agency register. Hook & Son (East Sussex) and similar are well-known. Sold direct, not via retail.

## Labels to avoid

- "UHT", "long-life", "extended shelf-life", "fresh-keeping", "longer-lasting" — all variants of ultra-pasteurised
- Any milk on the ambient shelf rather than the chiller
- Any bottle with a sell-by date more than ~12 days out (often = ultra-pasteurised)

## Cost reality check (6 L cheddar batch)

| Source | Approx cost |
|---|---|
| Tesco/Sainsbury's standard whole | ~£6 |
| Waitrose non-homogenised | ~£9 |
| Channel Island doorstep | ~£12 |
| Raw milk farm-direct | ~£10–15 |

So roughly **+£3–9 per batch** for milk that meaningfully changes the outcome. Cheap insurance on a 6-hour make.

## Recommended next steps by cheese type

- **Cheddar**: non-homogenised Channel Island or Jersey from Waitrose / Ocado / doorstep. Single change of "non-homogenised + Jersey" addresses two of the three big issues from a first batch with standard supermarket milk (wet curd from homogenisation, low fat-driven flavour).
- **Mozzarella**: any non-homogenised standard pasteurised whole. Mozzarella is more forgiving of homogenisation (it's a wet cheese by design), so the priority is just non-homogenisation. Channel Island would be lovely but isn't necessary.
- **Soft-ripened / lactic** (when you make them): non-homogenised is mandatory — homogenised milk gives a sticky, slimy curd that won't drain. Channel Island richness is gilding.
- **Hard aged styles** (Parmesan, Gruyère): raw or vat-pasteurised non-homogenised; standard supermarket milk really struggles for these.
