## 2026-05-16 — Cheddar (6 L)

- **Recipe:** [cheddar](recipes/cheddar.md)
- **Milk:** 6.0 L whole cow's milk, store-bought, pasteurised, **homogenised** (standard supermarket; not UHT)
- **Culture:** Chr Hansen R704, **0.064 g** (= 0.843 U, 6.7 % over book target of 0.060 g / 0.79 U). 50 DU pack measured at 3.796 g → 75.92 mg/U.
- **Rennet:** 1.58 mL liquid 1:10,000, to be diluted in ~30 mL cool boiled water
- **Other adds:** CaCl₂ 32 % solution, **1.2 mL** (= 0.2 mL/L), diluted in a splash of water, stirred in pre-rennet.

### Timeline
- 14:30 — start, milk into pot from fridge, slow warm
- 15:15 — milk at 32 °C; culture in. Ripening clock starts. (pH₀ recorded once reading stabilised — see below.)
- ~16:?? — temperature drifted to 33–34 °C while unattended, then brought back to 32 °C. No apparent ill effects.
- 17:45 — pH reads 6.40 (= pH₀ − 0.10, trigger met). Rennet diluted in ~30 mL cool boiled water, stirred in for ~30 s, then still. Set begins. Temp 32 °C.
- 18:15 — 30 min into set. Mature coagulation visible: curds pulling away from pan sides, whey starting to collect. Initial floc moment not directly observed (likely happened earlier, somewhere in the 15–25 min window). Holding through 45-min set.
- 18:30 — clean-break test: curd still custardy, sticking to knife. Not ready. Extended set; plan to retest at 19:00.
- ~18:45 — **heat-on-no-stir incident.** Heat had been left on under the unstirred gel to hold 32 °C; bottom of pan went scalding-hot while thermometer (mid/top) still read 32 °C. Heat off, pan moved to cool surface. Bottom milk layer likely briefly at 45–55 °C (possibly 60–70 °C very locally — see induction note below) — cultures there probably killed, proteins partially denatured. **This effectively started the scald early and unevenly.**
- 19:15 — cut over 5 min into ~1.5 cm cubes (larger than the book's ~1 cm, more forgiving for the weak gel). Clean break still imperfect — "not perfect but it'll do". Pan's latent heat carried the bulk temperature up to **40 °C** = scald target reached abruptly, no ramp needed.
- ~19:25 — settle done; start of stir-and-cook at 40 °C. Gentle stirring every 5 min, induction off / lowest setting to hold temp.
- ~19:30 — pH reads **6.20** (= 0.30 total drop from pH₀). Faster than during ripening — culture is more active post-cut (more biomass, more surface area, warmer temp).
- 20:40 — pH reads **6.00**. Drain trigger met (meter target 6.00; true ~6.20–6.30). Stirring stopped, curds settled.
- 20:45 — whey poured off. Whey **pH 5.90** (meter) = true ~6.10–6.20, **at the lower end of recipe drain window**. Slightly later than ideal but in range. Curds pushed to one side, pan propped to drain. Cheddaring clock starts.
- 21:30 — first cheddaring reading: meter **5.80** (0.10 drop in 45 min = ~0.033/15 min). Far below book rate (~0.15/15 min). Curd block had cooled toward room temperature.
- ~21:45 — bain-marie set up (kettle-hot water in a larger vessel) to bring curd back to ~32 °C and re-activate the surviving culture.
- 22:00 — meter **5.50** (0.30 drop in 30 min = 0.15/15 min — briefly at book rate). Acid acceleration from a combination of (a) warming back up and (b) normal cheddaring acid-concentration kicking in.
- Cheddaring carried out as a single matted block rather than the traditional cut-and-stack — block knitted but not properly slabbed. Turning continued every 15 min through the period; less whey expulsion than a full cheddaring would give.
- 23:25 — meter **5.30** (0.20 drop in 85 min = ~0.035/15 min). Acid stalled again — curd cooling, acid product inhibition setting in, or both.
- 00:20 — meter **5.20** (0.10 drop in 55 min). True pH ≈ 5.40–5.45 with offset — **squarely in book mill window**. Mill trigger.
- ~00:25 — milled by hand into ~2.5 × 5 cm chunks. Curd weight after milling: **859 g** (= 13.9 % of milk weight, well above book's 8–10 % expectation; reflects retained moisture from incomplete cheddaring).
- **Salt: 17.2 g total at 2 % of milled curd weight, applied in two halves of 8.6 g, 5 min apart, mixed gently.**
- ~00:40 — mellow done, curd into cheesecloth-lined mould (~100 mm tomme). **First press on at 2.5 kg (quarter-bucket equivalent).** Run for 1 h (shortened from book's 1–2 h to allow sleep).
- 01:40 — first press off. Cheese turned out, flipped, cheesecloth pulled taut to remove creases, back into mould. **Second press on at 10 kg (full bucket).** Plan: 12 h, off after ~14:00 next afternoon.
- (next day, ~14:00+) — second press off after 12 h. Pressed wheel: **748 g, ~100 mm diameter** (standard wheel size for the mould used). Whey expelled in presses: **111 g** (12.9 % of milled curd weight).
- (next day) — bandage applied using **coconut oil** in place of lard (50 g; same process: melt, soak muslin, apply 2 circles + 1 strip, set under ~1–2 kg for 30 min while it solidifies). Coconut oil is solid below 24 °C and will hold at cave temp; slight coconut note will migrate into the outer few mm of rind. Muslin sized by eye against the wheel. **Wrap quality: scrappy** — wrinkles, possible thin spots, edges not perfectly sealed. Patched bare areas and brushed extra oil over suspect spots. **Plan to re-bandage at month 1–2** if it looks shabby on inspection.
- (next day) — into the cheese fridge at ~12–15 °C inside a humidity micro-cave: food-safe plastic box with damp sponge, cheese on a small grid, lid slightly cracked. Target RH 75–85 % (bandaged cheese tolerates the lower end better than natural rind).

### Observations
- **Meter uncalibrated.** Buffers not run. Cold-milk reference earlier read **6.2**; warm milk stabilised at **6.5** (fresh pasteurised milk should be 6.5–6.8). Initial 6.4 reading at 15:15 was before the probe had settled.
  - Working assumption: meter reads **~0.2–0.3 low**, based on warm reading 6.5 vs assumed true ~6.7–6.8.
  - Relative changes (rennet trigger −0.10 from pH₀; cheddaring rate −0.15/15 min) are offset-immune — use as-written.
  - Absolute gates need correcting. Provisional thresholds on meter readings:
    - **Rennet trigger:** meter = **6.40** (= 6.50 − 0.10)
    - **Drain whey:** meter ≈ **6.00** (recipe true target 6.20–6.30)
    - **Mill curd:** meter ≈ **5.15** (recipe true target 5.40–5.45)
    - Bias toward earlier (slightly higher meter reading) if in doubt — under-acid is recoverable, over-acid isn't.
- pH₀ as-read (stabilised): **6.50**. Estimated true: ~6.70–6.80.
- **Slow ripening: 150 min** (15:15 → 17:45) to reach the 0.10 pH drop. Book target 60 min; recipe window 45–90 min; this exceeded the upper end by 60 min. Culture is working, but slowly. Candidate causes: pack viability (freezer cycling, time since opening), temperature wobble during ripening, possible meter noise masking a slightly earlier real trigger. Expect downstream stages to also run slow — drive everything by pH/visual gates, not the clock.
- **Slow set + early scald.** Set was still custardy at 45 min. While extending the set, the pan was heated unstirred to hold temp — created a vertical temperature gradient with the bottom layer at scald temperatures (~45–55 °C estimated) while the thermometer at mid/top still showed 32 °C. Implications:
  - Bottom-layer culture likely dead → expect downstream acidification to be even slower than the already-slow trajectory.
  - Effective "start of scald" is now in the pan, not at the post-cut step. Temperature distribution is uneven and must be equalised by the cutting + gentle stirring action.
  - Subsequent +2 °C / 15 min ramp to 40 °C should start from whatever even temperature is reached after settling — likely 34–36 °C, so fewer ramp steps needed.
  - Risk of stuck/scorched milk solids on pan bottom; inspect when draining.
- **Induction hob context.** Heating is via the pan base directly (energy induced into the steel). Heat is concentrated and fast-rising at the base — gradient between base and bulk forms much more steeply than on gas/coil. Made the heat-on-no-stir incident worse than it would have been on a slower hob: bottom-layer milk likely 60–70 °C briefly, with a high risk of cooked/stuck milk solids on the pan base. Watch for this when cleaning.
- **Acid trajectory through stir-and-cook.** From rennet at 17:45 (meter 6.40) to drain at 20:40 (meter 6.00) = 0.40 drop in 175 min, or ~0.034 pH / 15 min averaged. Recipe expected ~0.15 / 15 min during the *cheddaring* phase; the stir+cook phase is naturally slower. The culture is producing acid — just on the slow side.
- **Cheddaring temperature.** Curd cooled to room temperature within ~45 min of drain and acid generation crashed. Bain-marie warming reactivated the culture briefly but didn't sustain. The book's "falling temperature" through cheddaring really means falling *from* ~38–40 °C *toward* 22 °C *over* the cheddaring period — not falling to ambient in the first hour. Need an actively-maintained warm environment (bain-marie, oven on lowest setting, insulated box) to keep the curd around 30–35 °C through cheddaring.
- **Cheddaring technique.** Did not properly cut the matted curd into slabs and stack — just turned the single block every 15 min. Cheese will be wetter and less stratified than the book intends; closer to a young farmhouse cheddar than a traditional truckle.
- **Yield: 13.9 % from milled curd** (859 g from 6.18 kg milk). Recipe expected 8–10 %; the excess is water retention from incomplete cheddaring and homogenised milk. Expect significant whey expulsion in the two presses → finished wheel likely 650–720 g (11–12 %).
- **Final pressed yield: 12.1 % (748 g).** Slightly above prediction; presses expelled 111 g of whey (~13 % of milled weight) but the cheese stays at the wet end of the cheddar range. Consistent with the incomplete cheddaring narrative.
- **Total make time: ~11 h 10 min** start to second press (14:30 → 01:40), vs the recipe's "active time ~5.5–6 h + 1–2 h first press = ~7–8 h before stepping away". Today ran ~3 h over because of the slow ripening, extended set, and slow cheddaring.
- **Lessons for next time:**
  1. Never leave heat on under an unstirred gel — use a water bath, hot-water-bottle wrap, or off-on bursts with brief stirs early in the set.
  2. On induction especially, treat the pan base as the heating element — gradients form fast, so stir often or use a bain-marie.
  3. Place a second thermometer probe near the bottom if direct heating is unavoidable.
  4. Trust the book's 45-min set call — extending it added complexity without clear benefit.
  5. **Set up a bain-marie or insulated warm zone for the cheddaring step**, before starting the make. Curd needs ~32 °C through the cheddaring window, not ambient.
  6. **Cut the matted curd into proper slabs and stack** at start of cheddaring, even at small scale. Cleaner whey expulsion, better-defined texture.
  7. **Calibrate the pH meter before next make.** Buffers (4.00 + 7.00) take 10 min and remove a large layer of uncertainty.
  8. **Plan for a 10–12 h day at minimum** for cheddar, not the recipe's 7–8 h optimistic timing — at least until the process is well-grooved.
  9. **Bandage practice**: pre-cut muslin to size before melting the oil; lay out a clean workspace; apply with the cheese still slightly warm (rind tackier, cloth adheres better). First attempt was scrappy — worth a dry run with offcuts and butter next time.

### Outcome
- **Yield**: 748 g from 6.0 L (= 12.1 % of milk weight). Above book range (8–10 %) due to retained moisture.
- **Wheel**: ~100 mm diameter, lard-bandage style finish (coconut oil substituted).
- **Maturation**: in cheese fridge ~12–15 °C, in a humidity micro-cave (plastic box + damp sponge), target 75–85 % RH.
- **First taste**: planned month 3 (mid-August 2026). Expected window 3–5 months.

## 2026-05-21 — Mozzarella (4 L)

- **Recipe:** [mozzarella](recipes/mozzarella.md)
- **Milk:** 4.0 L whole cow's milk, Waitrose, **non-homogenised**, pasteurised
- **Culture:** Chr Hansen TCC-20, **0.072 g** (= 0.81 U, 1.62 % of 50 U pack; pack measured at 4.471 g → 89.42 mg/U)
- **Rennet:** 0.88 mL liquid 1:10,000, to be diluted in ~15 mL cool boiled water
- **Other adds:** CaCl₂ 32 % solution, **0.8 mL** (= 0.2 mL/L for non-homogenised), in pre-warming.

### Timeline
- ~12:30 — start (approx). Milk and CaCl₂ into pot, slow warm. pH meter calibrated this morning (was ~0.1 out, now within calibration).
- 13:05 — milk at 36 °C; **culture in**. Ripening clock starts. Temperature strategy: hold within 35–39 °C band (better-suited to induction than chasing exactly 37).
- 14:05 — end of 1 h ripening. Temp 37 °C ±0.5 °C; pH 6.6 (probe fully settled). **Rennet in**, stirred 30 s, then still. Set begins.
- 14:35 — 30 min into set. No clear flocculation signs visible yet (book says ~25 min). Held the line, lid on, no probing.
- 14:55 — clean break test: custardy, not quite as firm as ideal but workable for the directional-then-whisk cut. Started directional cut at 2 cm.
- 15:10 — first cut done; switched to whisk to finish to ~1 cm cubes.
- 15:25 — whisk-finish done. Settle 5 min. Bain-marie pre-prepped.
- 15:30 — **drain** into muslin-lined colander. Drain pH **6.5**. Colander into bain-marie (~50 °C water) immediately. Turning every 15–20 min from here.
- 17:00 — pH **5.6** (whey running off). Acidification rate ~0.15 pH/15 min — book rate exactly. Faster than predicted given the lowish pH₀.
- 17:35 — pH **5.1** (overshoot from 5.25 target; 0.5 drop in 35 min = ~0.21 pH/15 min, accelerated from earlier). **Mill trigger met (over).** Stretch test OK.
- ~17:40 — milled curd weighed: **683 g** (= **16.6 %** of milk weight; well above book's 10–12 %).
- ~17:45 — salt added: **10.2 g** at 1.5 % of milled curd, mixed gently.
- ~17:50 — **stretch**: ~683 mL of 95 °C water poured over salted milled curd in heatproof bowl. Stirred until single mass. Hand-shaped into pieces, re-dipped 1–2 times to reform solid balls.
- 18:00 — **3 balls** of similar size (~228 g each) into cold-water bath, then into cool tap water in fridge for storage.

### Observations
- **pH₀ progression** as probe settled and milk warmed: 6.0 → 6.1 → 6.2 (cold, settling) → 6.4 (warm, settling) → **6.6** (fully settled at rennet-in). True pH₀ was always ~6.6 — early readings were probe lag, not low milk pH. Lesson reconfirmed (from cheddar): wait for probe to fully stabilise before recording.
- **Ingredients pre-weighed/measured to within ~5 %** of target. Big improvement on cheddar batch.
- **Set was custardy at 50 min** — softer than ideal but workable for mozzarella's whisk-finish cutting. Non-homogenised + 0.2 mL/L CaCl₂ gave a working set; bumping to 0.3 mL/L for non-homogenised may be unnecessary (saved that for homogenised milk only).
- **Acidification was fast.** Drain at 15:30 (pH 6.5) → mill at 17:35 (pH 5.1) = 2 h 5 min, well inside the book's 4-hour expectation. Rate accelerated as pH dropped (0.15/15 min mid-phase, 0.21/15 min late). Mill caught at 5.1 rather than 5.25 — overshoot.
- **Yield 16.6 %** (683 g milled curd from 4.12 kg milk). Far above book's 10–12 % — non-homogenised whole milk retains more fat and water than the homogenised default the book assumed. Balls will be wetter and richer than book intent.
- **Stretch worked** but needed 1–2 re-dips per ball to consolidate. Consistent with mill pH being at the low edge of acceptable (5.05–5.10 borderline).
- **Total time start → balls in cold water: ~5 h 30 min** (12:30 → 18:00). Faster than the book's 6–7 h forecast.
- **Lessons for next time:**
  1. **Stretch-test much earlier.** Once pH drops below 5.6, test every 5–10 min, not 10–20. Window from 5.5 to 5.1 took only 35 min — very narrow.
  2. **0.2 mL/L CaCl₂ for non-homogenised milk was sufficient.** Don't bump to 0.3 unless using homogenised.
  3. **Set may be softer than expected at 50 min** for mozzarella; the whisk-finish cut is forgiving — no need to extend the set significantly.
  4. **Expect higher yield from non-homogenised milk** (16 % here vs 12 % book) — plan more storage capacity, more salt, more stretch water.
  5. **The bain-marie strategy worked.** Culture stayed active throughout acidification. Direct application of cheddar batch's hardest-won lesson.

### Outcome
- **Yield**: 683 g from 4.0 L (16.6 % of milk weight). 3 balls at ~228 g each.
- **Stretch quality**: smooth, slightly soft. Needed re-dipping during shaping.
- **Storage decision**: all 3 balls into cheese fridge at 13 °C in sealed container with cool tap water. Plan to move to main fridge (~4 °C) around 09:00 next morning (= ~15 h at cellar temp). Cellar storage benefits texture; shortened window reflects elevated spoilage risk from low mill pH (5.1) and high yield (lots of water in curd).
- **Expected eating plan given low mill pH:**
  - Day 0 (tonight): one ball at peak — Caprese / plain torn with olive oil + salt.
  - Day 1: second ball. Still fresh, slightly tangier.
  - Day 2: third ball, cooked applications (pizza, pasta bake).
  - Don't push past Day 3.
- **Storage watch-list**: sour smell beyond yogurty, slimy surface, ropy water, pink/orange tint → bin. Taste a small corner first from Day 2 onwards.
- **Tasted on YYYY-MM-DD**: TBD
- **What to change next time**: see Lessons above. Primary takeaway: test stretch from pH 5.6 onwards every 5–10 min — the window closes fast. Secondary: 0.2 mL/L CaCl₂ for non-homogenised milk was sufficient (no need to bump to 0.3).
