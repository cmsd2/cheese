# Cheddar

**Source:** *Home-made Cheese*, Paul Thomas.

A traditional pressed, dry-salted, cheddared-curd cow's-milk cheese. Aged 3+ months.

## At a glance

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Milk | Whole cow's milk |
| Culture | Mesophilic, predominantly homofermentative |
| Coagulation | Animal rennet, 1:10,000 strength |
| Curd handling | Cheddared (matted, milled), dry-salted |
| Press | Hard, increasing pressure |
| Affinage | 10–13 °C, 85 % RH, **3+ months** minimum |

## Ingredients

Book column is the recipe as written (11.4 L / 3 US gal). "Today" is scaled for **6 L** (factor 0.5263). Calculations:

```
scale         = 6.0 / 11.4 = 0.5263
milk mass     = 6.0 × 1.03 = 6.18 kg
salt @ 2% w/w of ~9.5 % yield curd:
              = 6.18 × 0.095 × 0.02 × 1000 ≈ 11.7 g
rennet        = 3.0 × 0.5263 ≈ 1.58 mL
Chr Hansen    = 1.5 × 0.5263 ≈ 0.79 U
Danisco       = 0.7 × 0.5263 ≈ 0.37 DCU
```

### Dosing R704 from a 50 DU packet

You have one 50 DU foil packet, enough for 500 L at Chr Hansen's standard 1 U/10 L (= 1 % bulk). The book is **32 % hotter** than that standard:

```
book rate     = 1.5 U / 11.4 L = 0.132 U/L (= 1.32 % bulk)
Chr Hansen    = 50 U / 500 L   = 0.100 U/L (= 1.00 % bulk)
```

**Measured pack contents: 3.796 g for 50 U → 0.07592 g/U (75.92 mg/U).**

For a 6 L batch:

| Rate | U needed | % of 50 DU pack | Mass to weigh | One pack covers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Book (1.32 % bulk) | 0.79 U | 1.58 % | **60.0 mg** | 380 L = **63 × 6 L batches** |
| Chr Hansen std (1.00 % bulk) | 0.60 U | 1.20 % | 45.6 mg | 500 L = 83 × 6 L batches |

Measuring 1.58 % of a small foil pack at home — three options, best first:

1. **Weigh the powder** (0.01 g scale). Weigh the unopened packet, calculate 1.58 % of the gross-minus-foil weight, tip out that mass. Most accurate. Re-seal and freeze the remainder.
2. **Reconstitute and aliquot.** Tip the whole pack into 100 mL chilled, recently-boiled water; this gives **0.5 U/mL**; dose **1.58 mL** by syringe. Must be used within a few hours — losing viability after that — so only useful when you're making several batches close together.
3. **Re-package once.** Open the pack indoors away from drafts, weigh-divide into 10 small foil envelopes (each = 5 DU = ~30 L of milk at std rate), heat-seal, freeze. Then for each batch open one mini-pack and weigh out a smaller fraction. Most practical if you'll be making many cheeses from this pack.

R704 stores well **frozen and unopened** for the labelled shelf life; once opened, viability drops, so package and re-freeze the remainder promptly.

## Targets — temperature and pH

| Stage | Temp (°C) | pH | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-culture (record) | 32 | record | Baseline before inoculation |
| Ripening | 32 | drift down | **1 hour**, stir occasionally |
| Rennet addition | 32 | — | Stir in, then still for **45 min** total |
| Coagulation signs | 32 | — | Expect at 15–30 min in |
| Cutting curd | 32 | — | Cubes; cut slowly over 5 min, settle 5 min |
| Scalding | 32 → 40 | — | **+2 °C every 15 min**, stir every 5 min, ~1 h |
| Whey drain | 40 | **6.2–6.3** | Record actual pH |
| Cheddaring | falling | 6.2 → 5.40–5.45 | Knit, flip every 15 min; **0.15 pH per 15 min** = ~75–90 min total |
| Milling | falling | **5.40–5.45** | Mill into 2.5 × 5 cm chunks |
| Dry salting | falling | ~5.4 | 2 % w/w of milled curd, in two halves 5 min apart |
| Mellow | falling to 22 | — | 15 min minimum; continue until curd hits **22 °C** |
| Pre-press | **22** | — | Critical — higher loses fat, lower fails to knit |
| First press | 22 | — | 1–2 h at ~2.5 kg (quarter bucket) |
| Second press | room | — | 12 h at ~10 kg (full bucket), flipped, cloth re-tightened |
| Bandage | room | — | Lard-soaked muslin, then brief press to set |
| Aging | **12–15** | ~5.0 entry | 5–6 months book recipe; **3–5 months expected** for our 550 g wheel. Turn weekly, wipe surface moulds. |

Book uses a **fixed 45-min set time** rather than flocculation-multiplier cutting. If coagulation signs appear at 15 min that's an implicit floc multiplier of ~3 (classic hard-cheese); at 30 min it's ~1.5 (low). Note your coagulation time in the log — if it consistently floc's late (>25 min) the rennet dose may be light.

Milling pH **5.40–5.45** is at the high/mild end of the cheddar window (forum sources cite 5.2–5.4 as the canonical range). This book is aimed at a milder, sweeter cheddar; expect a less sharp result than a textbook traditional.

## Decision rules — pH over clock

The book gives times. Times are a proxy. **The actual signal is pH** — drive every transition by pH where one is given, by visible curd state otherwise.

### Why

- The "1 hour ripening" assumes a specific bacterial population active at 32 °C. Under-dosed or partially-viable culture → same job, more time needed.
- The whole make is pH-gated downstream (drain 6.2–6.3; mill 5.40–5.45). If acidification is slow at the top, it'll be slow at the bottom — but the right *endpoints* are still hit.

### Asymmetric risk

- **Under-dose is recoverable.** Acid is behind — stop stirring, let curds settle, give them time. Hit the target pH eventually.
- **Over-dose is dangerous.** Acid arrives before moisture is out of the curd — "runaway acidity", few good levers.

→ When the dose is uncertain, **err slightly low**, not high.

### Trigger windows for the ripening step

| t (from culture-in) | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–45 min | Don't rennet yet — let freeze-dried cells fully rehydrate even if dose is hot |
| 45–90 min | Watch pH; rennet at the **0.1 drop** from pH₀ |
| > 90 min, no drop | Something is wrong — dead culture, milk too cold, contamination. Investigate, don't push on |

The book's 60 min is the *target* — being earlier or later by 20–30 min is normal.

### Trade-offs in one paragraph

Culture quantity sets the starting population. Time and temperature let it multiply (~30–60 min doubling). You can substitute one for another: less culture + more time ≈ more culture + less time. **Don't raise temperature to compensate** — R704 is mesophilic and stressing it changes flavour and strain proportions. Just wait.

## Process — book steps

Steps 1–14 are transcribed from *Home-made Cheese* (Paul Thomas). **Pressing, drying, and ageing steps are not yet captured — paste when ready.**

1. **Heat** milk to **32 °C** and record the pH.
2. **Inoculate** with R704 (today: **0.79 U** ≈ 1.58 % of the 50 DU pack). Leave for **~1 hour**, stirring occasionally to incorporate any cream rising. **Trigger rennet on the 0.1 pH drop from pH₀**, not the clock — see Decision rules above. Expect this between 45 and 90 min.
3. **Add rennet** (today: **1.58 mL** of 1:10,000), stir in, then leave to stand **45 min** at 32 °C. Coagulation signs should appear between **15 and 30 min**.
4. **Test the curd** (clean break / floc-check), then **cut to cubes** slowly over **5 min**. Leave to settle **5 min**.
5. **Stir** every 5 min for **1 hour**, raising the temperature from 32 °C to **40 °C in ~2 °C increments** (i.e. roughly +2 °C every 15 min). By the end the curds will have shrunk into firm, compact pellets.
6. **Stop stirring** and let the curds sink below the whey for **5 min**.
7. **Pour off the whey** and record the pH — should be **6.2–6.3**.
8. **Push the curds to one side** of the pan and prop it so whey drains. Leave **15 min** — the curds will start to knit.
9. **Test the pH** of the whey running off.
10. **Turn the curd block every 15 min**, recording pH each time. Expect a drop of **~0.15 pH per 15 min**. When it reaches **5.40–5.45** it is ready to mill. Normally **~75–90 min** from drain (book: "over an hour").
11. **Weigh the curd**. Compute **2 % of that weight** in salt — write it down in the log; don't trust the pre-batch table figure.
12. **Mill** by hand into chunks **~2.5 × 5 cm**. Texture should resemble cooked chicken-breast layers.
13. **Sprinkle half the salt** over the curds and mix; wait **5 min**; then add the **second half** and mix.
14. **Mellow** 15 min.

15. **Pre-press temperature check** — curd should be at **22 °C** before going into the mould. Hotter than 22 °C and you lose fat through the cloth; cooler and the curds won't knit. Adjust mellow length / ambient to hit it.
16. **First press — 1 to 2 hours.** Pack the curds into a cheesecloth-lined mould. Apply pressure equivalent to a **quarter-bucket of water** (≈ 2.5 kg, assuming a 10 L bucket).
17. **Second press — 12 hours.** Remove from press, turn over and pull the cloth taut to remove creases. Apply a **full bucket** of water (≈ 10 kg) and leave overnight.
18. **Trim.** Remove from press, turn out, trim edges tidy.
19. **Lard-bandage.** Melt the lard. Cut **2 muslin circles** ~20 mm larger than the wheel diameter, and **1 rectangular strip** long enough to wrap the side with overlap. Soak in the melted fat. Smooth a circle onto top and bottom, then wrap the side strip overlapping both.
20. **Set the bandage.** Return to the mould and press lightly while the fat cools and solidifies (locking the bandage in place).
21. **Mature** in a cool larder or cheese fridge at **12–15 °C** for **5–6 months**. (A traditional 23 kg truckle takes ~9 months; smaller home wheels mature faster.) **Turn the wheel weekly** and **wipe off any natural mould** that appears on the surface.

### Maturation note for the 6 L batch

Our ~550 g wheel is much smaller than the "smaller cheeses" the book has in mind (typically 1–2 kg home wheels). Crude surface-area-to-volume scaling:

```
SA/V ∝ m^(-1/3)
(23000 g / 550 g)^(1/3) = 3.47×   → 9 months / 3.47 ≈ 2.6 months
(23000 g / 1000 g)^(1/3) = 2.84×  → 3.2 months
(23000 g / 2000 g)^(1/3) = 2.26×  → 4.0 months
```

So our 550 g wheel reaches moisture-loss equivalence in **~2.6 months**, but flavour development needs time independent of drying out. The book's 5–6 months for ~1–2 kg wheels is slower than pure SA/V would predict — they're leaving headroom for flavour. For our small wheel, **start tasting at 3 months**, expect "ready" between **3 and 5 months**.

### Maturation by age tier (small home wheels, ~700–800 g)

For a bandaged home wheel in this size range, ageing windows compress dramatically vs the book's 1–2 kg targets. SA/V scaling does the moisture work fast; flavour develops on a more linear curve.

| Stage | Age | What to expect | Comparable to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Too young** | 0–8 weeks | Squeaky, milky, pH-acid sharp. More cottage cheese than cheddar. | — |
| **OK** | 2–3 months | Recognisably cheddar, mild. Texture starting to firm. First flavour notes (buttery, slight tang). | Supermarket "mild cheddar" |
| **Good** | 3–5 months | Sweet spot for most small wheels. Clear cheddar character, tangy without being sharp. Texture firm, not crumbly. | Decent mid-range farmhouse |
| **Best** | 5–7 months | Peak flavour-to-texture balance for this size. Tang deepened, slight nuttiness. May see calcium lactate crystals starting. | Good "mature cheddar" |
| **Past prime** | 7–9 months | Risk of crumbling, drying-out, or surface-mould issues winning. Bandage may be cracking. | Variable — could be excellent or wrecked |
| **Don't bother** | 9+ months | Wheel is likely overdried. Big-truckle ageing techniques don't scale here. | — |

**Tasting plan**: cut a small wedge at **month 3** (reference point — tells you where acidification landed), again at **month 4–5** (most likely sweet spot), and **month 6–7** (final taste; may be peak if texture is still firm-not-crumbly). Don't push past **8 months** unless the wheel feels extraordinarily right.

**After each tasting cut, seal the exposed face immediately** — either wax it, or wrap tightly in cling film. An open face dries rapidly and ruins the rest of the wheel.

**Adjustments by batch character**:
- **Wet curd** (incomplete cheddaring / homogenised milk) → buys headroom on the early end; can push toward upper "Best" range before drying becomes a problem.
- **Mild starting pH** (mill at 5.40+ true) → flavour lands "mild and rich" rather than "sharp and tangy". Reduce expectations of classic-cheddar pungency.
- **Scrappy bandage** → watch from month 2 onwards; re-bandage if cracks or lifts appear.
- **Coconut-oil bandage** (vs lard) → slight antimicrobial bonus, marginally cleaner rind through the window.

### Rind care

- **Turn weekly** — keeps moisture distribution even, prevents flat-side moisture pooling.
- **Wipe moulds** with a brine-damp or vinegar-damp cloth (the book doesn't specify; brine ~3 % salt or 5 % white-wine vinegar are typical home choices). The lard-bandage protects against most surface growth but won't stop it entirely in a humid cave.
- If the bandage cracks or lifts, patch with more melted lard.

### Pressing pressures

The book measures pressure in "buckets of water". A standard UK household bucket holds ~10 L (10 kg full). Working that out for a ~100 mm diameter wheel:

```
mould area = π × (100/2)² mm² = 78.5 cm² = 12.17 sq in
quarter bucket (2.5 kg) → 5.5 lb / 12.17 sq in = 0.45 PSI
full bucket    (10 kg)  → 22 lb  / 12.17 sq in = 1.81 PSI
```

| Stage | Bucket | Approx weight | PSI on 100 mm wheel |
|---|---|---|---|
| First press (1–2 h) | quarter | 2.5 kg | 0.45 |
| Second press (12 h) | full | 10 kg | 1.81 |
| Setting bandage | light (book unspecified) | ~1–2 kg | 0.18–0.36 |

These are at the gentle end — comparable to Ricki Carroll's 10 lb / 20 lb cheddar recipe (0.82 / 1.64 PSI). If you have a heavier or lighter bucket, scale proportionally to keep the same kg.

## Notes & open questions

- **Book rate vs Chr Hansen standard.** The book dose (1.32 % bulk) is 32 % hotter than Chr Hansen's standard recommendation (1.00 % bulk) and well below the 2 % the cheeseforum sources quote as typical for cheddar. Possible explanations: (1) the book is deliberately running slow for more flavour development; (2) the recipe assumes raw or very fresh milk with native flora helping; (3) the forum's 2 % figure is for faster commercial makes. **Plan:** start at the book's 0.79 U for the first batch, observe acidity timing, adjust on the next batch if it's too slow or too fast.
- **Rennet IMCU dose.** 3 mL of 1:10,000 over 11.4 L ≈ 0.26 mL/L. If your rennet is labelled ~200 IMCU/mL (typical modern "single strength" liquid), that's ~**52 IMCU/L** — mid-range, fine for a 30–40 min set. Recompute using your bottle's actual IMCU rating if it differs.
- **Salt quantity** assumes 9.5 % yield. Weigh the actual milled curd and apply 2 % w/w rather than using the table figure.
- **6 L mould.** Press into a mould sized for ~0.6 kg of curd; a 4-inch / 100 mm tomme mould or similar works.
