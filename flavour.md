# Flavour targeting

How to influence the flavour and texture profile of aged cheeses (primarily cheddar) through choices at the make and at the cave. Reference companion to [calculations.md](calculations.md) and [milk.md](milk.md).

## The biochemistry — three parallel breakdown processes

Cheese ageing is governed by enzyme-driven breakdown of three substrates. All three are happening simultaneously; what *dominates* depends on conditions.

| Process | What breaks down | Compounds produced | Flavour notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proteolysis** | Casein → peptides → free amino acids | Sulphur compounds, ammonia, brothy/savoury molecules | Savoury, brothy, sulphur, nutty, umami, "catty" |
| **Lipolysis** | Triglycerides → free fatty acids | Butyric, caproic, caprylic acids; methyl ketones | Sharp, tangy, soapy, "bitey", peppery |
| **Glycolysis** | Residual lactose → lactic acid (then breakdown) | Lactic acid, then aldehydes, ketones, lactones | Tang up front, then fruity/buttery/milky over time |

The enzymes responsible come from three sources:

- **Residual rennet** (chymosin) — keeps cleaving casein slowly throughout ageing
- **Starter culture bacteria and their enzymes** — even after the cells die, their proteases and lipases remain active
- **Adjunct cultures** (added deliberately at the make) — *L. helveticus*, *L. paracasei*, lipases

## Age-vs-character mapping

Reference age ranges are for **large traditional wheels (5–20 kg)**. For small home wheels (~700 g, our typical), expect peak character at roughly **half to a third** of these ages because of surface-area-to-volume scaling.

| Large-wheel age | Small-wheel equivalent | Flavour character |
|---|---|---|
| 2–3 months | 1–2 months | Mild, creamy, milky-sweet; barely beyond fresh |
| 4–6 months | 2–4 months | Emerging tang, slight nuttiness, soft texture |
| 9–12 months ("sharp") | 4–6 months | Tangy + nutty, firmer texture, complex |
| 18–24 months ("extra sharp") | 6–9 months | Crumbly, pronounced sharpness, sulphur/brothy notes, often with calcium lactate crystals |
| 2+ years ("vintage") | Risky on small wheels (dry out) | Very intense, complex, "catty", crystalline |

See [recipes/cheddar.md](recipes/cheddar.md) "Maturation by age tier" for the small-wheel-specific tasting plan.

## Levers at the make

| Lever | Sharper / more developed | Milder / softer |
|---|---|---|
| **Mill pH** | Lower (5.1–5.2) — more demineralisation, looser matrix | Higher (5.3–5.5) — tighter knit, milder |
| **Moisture content** | Lower (thorough cheddaring, drier curd) — concentrates flavour | Higher (wet cheddaring) — dilutes flavour, slows development |
| **Salt** | Higher (2.2–2.5 %) — slows further acid, controls ageing rate | Lower (1.5–2 %) — softer texture, faster early acidification, can over-acidify |
| **Cheddaring temperature** | 35–38 °C sustained — drives whey out, sets up drier curd | Allowed to cool — wetter, milder result |
| **Adjunct cultures** | *L. helveticus* → deeper nutty/umami; *L. paracasei* → fruity esters | Plain mesophilic (R704 alone) — clean lactic |
| **Lipase addition** | Calf/kid lipase → more lipolysis → pepperier, bitey character | None → cleaner, less complex |
| **Milk type** | Raw → native flora → most complex; non-homogenised whole → firm curd, full flavour | UHT/ultra-pasteurised → fails; standard pasteurised homogenised → milder |
| **Breed** | Channel Island (Jersey/Guernsey) → higher fat → richer flavour | Holstein → neutral baseline |

See [milk.md](milk.md) for milk-selection detail.

## Levers at the cave

| Lever | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | Warmer (14–15 °C) speeds all enzyme activity → faster flavour development; cooler (10–12 °C) slows everything → more graceful, predictable |
| **Humidity** | Higher (85–95 %) preserves moisture → less concentrated, longer to develop; lower (75–80 %) accelerates moisture loss → more concentrated flavour faster |
| **Wrapping** | Bandage (lard or coconut oil) → "open" complex character, slow oxidation contributes nutty notes; vacuum pack → "closed" anaerobic profile, cleaner, more straight-lactic |
| **Turning frequency** | Weekly turning evens moisture distribution; more frequent (twice weekly) for irregular wheels |
| **Time** | The main driver; can't be substituted. Patience is the cheese maker's primary skill |

See [recipes/cheddar.md](recipes/cheddar.md) "Maturation by age tier" and the bandage discussion. See `milk.md` for the bandage vs vacuum decision rationale.

## Targeting specific profiles — practical recipes

### Sharp / tangy traditional cheddar

The most common "wanted" profile.

- **Mill pH**: 5.1–5.2 (true) — at the low end of the recipe window
- **Salt**: 2.2–2.5 % of milled curd
- **Curd**: drier — proper slab-and-stack cheddaring, sustained 35–38 °C
- **Cave**: 12–14 °C, 80–85 % RH, bandaged
- **Age**: 5–7 months (small wheel) or 9–12 months (large wheel)
- **Don't go below**: mill pH true 5.05, or you'll get brittle and bitter

### Mild creamy "supermarket block" cheddar

- **Mill pH**: 5.4–5.5 (true) — high end of window
- **Salt**: 1.5–2 % of milled curd
- **Curd**: wetter is fine — simplified cheddaring (single block, turn only)
- **Cave**: 10–12 °C, can vacuum pack
- **Age**: 2–4 months (small wheel)
- **Eat young** — past 4 months on a small wheel and it goes downhill

### Nutty / umami-forward

- **Mill pH**: standard 5.3–5.4 — too low and tang dominates
- **Salt**: 2 %
- **Curd**: moderate moisture (some retained — substrate for proteolysis)
- **Adjunct culture**: *L. helveticus* if available, added at the make
- **Cave**: 13–15 °C, 80–85 % RH, bandaged
- **Age**: 6–9 months
- **Watch**: warm cave + bandaged needs more mould management

### Fruity / lactone-forward

- **Lipase**: small dose of calf or kid lipase at the make for lipolysis
- **Mill pH**: standard 5.3–5.4
- **Salt**: 2 %
- **Cave**: cool (10–11 °C), high humidity (85–90 %)
- **Age**: 4–6 months
- **Tend toward**: bandaged, not vacuum (oxygen helps lactone formation)

### Sulphur / brothy mature

- **Mill pH**: 5.1–5.2 (true)
- **Salt**: 2.5 %
- **Curd**: drier, more thoroughly cheddared
- **Adjunct culture**: *L. helveticus*
- **Cave**: warmer (14–15 °C), drier (75–80 % RH)
- **Age**: 6+ months for the sulphur notes to emerge
- **Hardest target** for home setups — needs adjuncts, sustained warm ageing, careful rind management

## Common failure modes by flavour outcome

| Outcome | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Sour / bitter / soapy | Mill pH too low (<5.0), or wet curd + low pH (concentrated acid) |
| Bland / no character | Wet curd + warm fast acidification + young age, OR pasteurised milk + no adjuncts |
| Ammonia / over-developed too early | Cave too warm, too humid, wheel too small for chosen age |
| Crumbly to point of falling apart | Mill pH too low + over-aged, or salt too high (>3 %) |
| Rancid / soapy-fishy | Excess lipolysis — too much lipase, or contamination, or stored too warm |
| Pink / off-coloured | Over-acidification + low calcium — usually unrecoverable; bin it |

## Reading the cheese as it ages

Tasting cuts at planned intervals tell you where the cheese is going:

- **Surface moisture / sweat** = humidity too high or wheel still acidifying internally
- **Cracks** = too dry, humidity too low or temperature too warm
- **Bandage mould** = humidity at the high end of OK — normal, manage by wiping
- **Sharp "bite" on tongue tip** = acid character developed (proteolysis/lipolysis active)
- **Lingering aftertaste** = good sign — complex compounds present
- **Numbing/burning** = excessive lipolysis (peppery FFAs) — over-aged or over-lipase'd
- **"Gloop" or pasty mouthfeel** = too wet for the age — drain pH was too high or cheddaring incomplete
- **Crunchy crystals (calcium lactate)** = appears at 5+ months on small wheels; sign of proper acid development
